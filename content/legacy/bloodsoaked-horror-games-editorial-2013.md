I keep buying horror games on disc because I need the object.

Not because I'm a luddite. Because horror works when something can be *taken away* — when a save file lives on a memory card you might lose, when a rental due date means you never finish the story, when the game exists in your house like a VHS you were afraid to rewind.

## The convenience trap

Steam libraries are graves. Thousands of titles you'll never open again, filed in a cloud you don't control. Horror needs weight. *Silent Hill 2* on a scratched PS2 disc feels different than *Silent Hill 2* in a bundle you bought for four dollars and forgot.

## What I'm asking

Not every game needs a steelbook. I'm asking developers and players to remember that fear lives in the body — in the late-night walk to the shelf, in the disc that won't read, in the object you can't delete with a right click.

Bloodsoaked Media exists partly to argue for that kind of fear. This editorial is early proof I meant it.
